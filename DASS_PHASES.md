# DASS Phase Operating Contract

DASS is the repository-native autonomous production system. Its achievement and live operation are judged against the governed runtime that can run from the repository/GitHub execution assets; a separate cloud/physical host is not a prerequisite for DASS.

## Purpose

DASS is operated as a two-phase autonomous workspace with an explicit achievement transition:

1. **PRE-DASS** — maximize progress toward DASS achievement while preserving safety, liveness, authority, measurement integrity, and useful preparation for the next phase.
2. **POST-DASS** — once DASS achievement is positively established, maximize the useful capability, resilience, autonomy, verified outcomes, and economic value of the live system.

The transition is state-driven, not operator-driven.

## Phase boundary

The authoritative DASS measurement remains the gate. DASS achievement means the declared production surface is fully mapped, pure, and at the configured 100% target, with no operational-looking unclassified paths and no quarantined imports.

DASS liveness is demonstrated by the governed GitHub-hosted autonomous production-service workflow actually executing the integrated supervisor and verifying its liveness/authority contract. A missing external DigitalOcean host does not invalidate DASS; it is an external deployment concern.

No post-DASS action may be treated as proof of DASS achievement. Post-DASS activation consumes the persisted achievement state produced by the authoritative measurement/transition mechanism.

## PRE-DASS workspace

While DASS is not achieved, autonomous work should preferentially:

- identify and remove the highest-value remaining DASS gaps;
- preserve fail-closed measurement and authority boundaries;
- maintain production liveness and recovery;
- test and verify changes before they become active;
- prepare post-DASS objectives, capabilities, metrics, and recovery paths without activating them prematurely;
- avoid expanding the DASS denominator merely to make the score easier to reach;
- avoid spending operator attention when the system can safely determine and execute the next step itself.

The optimization criterion is progress toward verified DASS achievement per unit of human attention and operational risk.

## POST-DASS workspace

After achievement, the autonomous objective changes. The system should continuously:

- discover useful opportunities and weaknesses;
- rank them by expected value, confidence, reversibility, cost, and risk;
- execute only through the governed authority path;
- verify real outcomes rather than assuming success;
- learn from outcomes and update future prioritization;
- improve reliability, autonomy, capability, and economic usefulness;
- retain rollback/recovery and safety constraints;
- periodically re-check DASS integrity so post-DASS evolution cannot silently bypass the original contract.

Post-DASS optimization is deliberately open-ended: achievement is a transition into continuous improvement, not the end of operation.

## Continuous preparation

PRE-DASS and POST-DASS concerns may be prepared concurrently, but activation is phase-gated. Post-DASS plans may be designed, tested, and staged before achievement; their live execution begins only after the achievement transition is positively established.

## Operator objective

Human involvement is an exception path. The autonomous system should diagnose, prioritize, implement, verify, recover, and continue whenever the applicable authority and safety policy permit it. The operator should primarily handle genuinely unavailable authority, ambiguous high-impact decisions, or failures that the system cannot safely recover from itself.

## Invariants

- DASS measurement remains fail-closed.
- The phase transition cannot be triggered by a self-asserted success flag alone.
- Quarantined legacy/income material cannot become an implicit DASS dependency.
- Production actions remain governed by the existing authority controls.
- Failed verification does not advance phase state.
- Post-DASS evolution must not weaken the transition contract.
- External infrastructure availability is not silently promoted into a DASS prerequisite.
